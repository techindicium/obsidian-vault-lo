# Aversive Stimulus Mechanism in Security Controls

> A behaviorist model — grounded in experimental psychology (behaviorism / experimental behavior analysis) — describing how a security or governance control (manual approval, compliance gate, access friction) functions as an **aversive stimulus** within a behavioral contingency (stimulus → behavior → consequence), reducing the likelihood of the desired behavior rather than merely blocking bad behavior.

## Origin

Developed by [[nelson-novaes-neto]] in his Master's thesis in Experimental Psychology (PUC-SP, ~2011), later published as the book *"A Internet como um laboratório para a análise do comportamento: Psicologia Experimental"* (with Sérgio V. de Luna), and empirically validated in the paper "The Effects of Security Controls on Human Behavior in Online Social Networks" — demonstrating that manipulating a security control in an online chat service measurably changes user behavior.

## Applied to AI governance: shadow AI

Applied to AI transformation: the more governance friction a new AI system imposes, the lower the spontaneous adoption by internal teams — the natural result is **shadow AI** (unofficial use of tools outside the bank's control), exactly the opposite of what the governance was meant to prevent.

## Why it matters strategically

This isn't a concept to introduce to [[nelson-novaes-neto]] — he empirically proved it before becoming CTO. The effective move is a calibrated question that leads him to apply his own thesis to the current problem, e.g.: "In your master's research you showed that security controls act as an aversive stimulus and reduce behavior — how are you thinking about that while designing AI governance gates, so they don't kill adoption by your own teams?"
