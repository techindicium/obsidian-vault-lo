# Never Split the Difference — Negotiation Tactics

> A set of tactical-empathy negotiation techniques from Chris Voss's *Never Split the Difference*, used to build trust and surface real leverage points in early relationship-building conversations, rather than negotiating on price or leading with a generic consulting pitch.

## Core techniques

- **Tactical empathy (labeling)** — name the other side's emotion or concern before they have to voice it, to disarm defensiveness. E.g., acknowledging that any external AI proposal has to pass the filter of a framework they personally authored.
- **Calibrated questions** — open-ended "how"/"what" questions instead of closed yes/no questions, so the other side can't simply shut the conversation down. See [[label-calibrated-question-rapport-tactic]] for the specific variant used with [[paulo-pituba]].
- **Accusation audit** — preemptively name what the other side might be thinking about you (e.g., "you've probably seen consultants show up without understanding X"), which disarms defensiveness before the meeting turns adversarial.
- **The black swan** — the hidden, non-obvious leverage point that changes the negotiation once uncovered. For [[nelson-novaes-neto]], the hypothesis is that his strongest emotional asset isn't the CTO title but authorship of his AI security framework — a project that amplifies that framework's visibility earns a much stronger internal sponsor than budget approval alone.
- **"That's right", not "you're right"** — the goal is to get the other party to say "that's exactly it" (confirming you truly understood their position), not a superficial "you're right" that just ends the conversation. Summarize their view with technical precision before proposing anything new.

## Applied examples

- [[paulo-pituba]] — near-total absence of public information required relying only on the label + calibrated question technique, anchored on his one verified fact (Rede → C6 career move).
- [[nelson-novaes-neto]] — richer public profile allowed the full toolkit: labeling his likely frustration with generic AI consulting pitches, calibrated questions about how his own framework is used day-to-day, and an accusation audit acknowledging he "literally wrote the playbook" on AI governance.
