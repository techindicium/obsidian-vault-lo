# ISO/IEC 42001:2023 AI Management System

> The international standard specifying requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an organizational AI Management System (AIMS).

## Description

[[aisvs-standard|AISVS]] cites ISO/IEC 42001 as a complementary governance framework: where AISVS provides testable, engineering-level security requirements, ISO 42001 provides the organizational management-system layer (policies, roles, continual improvement processes) that governs how AI is developed and operated more broadly — similar in scope role to how the [[nist-ai-risk-management-framework|NIST AI RMF]] is also cited.

> **Note on sourcing:** ISO does not publish the full standard text for free — `iso.org/standard/42001` returns only an abstract/purchase page (and blocks automated fetching outright). The content below is synthesized from independent secondary sources (RSI Security, Konfirmity) that agree on structure, rather than read directly from the primary text. Treat clause wording as paraphrased, not verbatim.

Published December 18, 2023 by ISO and IEC jointly, under the full title "Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Management system." It's the first AI-specific management-system standard, and — unlike the [[nist-ai-risk-management-framework|NIST AI RMF]] — it is **certifiable**: organizations can be audited and certified against it (3-year validity, annual surveillance audits), typically costing $20,000–$60,000 and taking 4-9 months (shorter for organizations already ISO 27001-certified).

## Clause Structure (4-10, Harmonized Structure)

Uses ISO's Harmonized Structure — the same clause numbering as ISO 27001 (information security) and other ISO management-system standards — so it integrates cleanly into an organization that already runs one of those.

- **Clauses 1-3** — Scope, normative references, terms and definitions (framework context, not requirements).
- **Clause 4 — Context of the Organization** — understanding the org's IT/risk environment, stakeholder needs, and AIMS scope.
- **Clause 5 — Leadership** — leadership commitment, AI policy, assigned roles/accountability.
- **Clause 6 — Planning** — risk and opportunity assessment (general + AI-specific + impact assessments), objectives for fairness/accuracy/security, change management.
- **Clause 7 — Support** — resourcing, competence, awareness, communication, documentation control.
- **Clause 8 — Operation** — operational planning around AI-specific risk, risk treatment/prioritization, understanding impact on broader systems.
- **Clause 9 — Performance Evaluation** — monitoring inputs/outputs, internal audits, management review.
- **Clause 10 — Improvement** — continual improvement, handling nonconformity.

## Annex A — 38 Controls Across 9 Objectives

- **A.2** — Establishing and reviewing the AI policy
- **A.3** — Roles, responsibilities, and reporting of AI concerns
- **A.4** — Documenting the data, tooling, compute, and human resources an AI system depends on
- **A.5** — Impact-assessment process on individuals and society
- **A.6** — Responsible design, development, verification, and deployment
- **A.7** — Data governance, quality, provenance, and preparation
- **A.8** — Transparency and documentation for users and affected parties
- **A.9** — Responsible and intended use, including monitoring in operation
- **A.10** — Allocating responsibility across the AI supply chain

Supporting annexes: B (implementation guidance), C (risk-source ideas), D (sector-specific application).

## Relationship to Other Frameworks

- **ISO 27001** — shares the Harmonized Structure; ISO 27001 certifies information security, ISO 42001 certifies AI management. Existing ISO 27001 certification reduces ISO 42001 effort by roughly a third to a half.
- **[[nist-ai-risk-management-framework|NIST AI RMF]]** — complementary, not competing: the AI RMF is voluntary and not certifiable, but work done against it carries directly into an ISO 42001 audit.
- **EU AI Act** — as of 2026, ISO 42001 is not a harmonized standard under the Act (no automatic "presumption of conformity"), but certification provides strong evidence of the governance the Act requires.
